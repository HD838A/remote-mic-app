# Glossary

This guide explains names that may appear during installation, device selection, macOS setup, logging, or technical troubleshooting. You normally do not need these details for everyday use.

## Remote and buttons

### Xiaomi Bluetooth Remote 2 Pro / RC003

**Plain-language explanation:** The Xiaomi Bluetooth voice remote supported by this app. RC003 is its model code in technical material and some device information.

**When you may see it:** Bluetooth device lists, product packaging, logs, or troubleshooting material.

### Fn

**Plain-language explanation:** A function modifier key on Mac keyboards. The app can let the remote's microphone button provide Fn behavior when needed.

**When you may see it:** Button instructions, shortcut recording, or keyboard settings.

### HID

**Plain-language explanation:** A standard macOS uses for keyboards, mice, and remote-control buttons. For everyday use, think of it as the button device or button data.

**When you may see it:** Console logs, technical material, or Input Monitoring troubleshooting.

## Microphones and audio

### Compatible microphone

**Plain-language explanation:** A virtual microphone designed for input methods or apps that require a particular kind of audio device.

**When you may see it:** Selecting voice output or installing the input-method compatibility driver.

### MiRemoteV 2ch

**Plain-language explanation:** The compatible microphone's actual name in the macOS audio device list.

**When you may see it:** Audio MIDI Setup, Sound settings, or the app's audio device picker.

### BlackHole

**Plain-language explanation:** An open-source virtual audio driver that passes audio between Mac apps.

**When you may see it:** Installing a virtual audio device, selecting voice output, or troubleshooting audio routing.

### Virtual microphone and audio loopback

**Plain-language explanation:** A virtual microphone supplies sound produced by one app to another app as microphone input. Audio loopback is the general method used to pass that sound between software.

**When you may see it:** Configuring voice input for an input method, recorder, meeting app, or streaming app.

### Core Audio

**Plain-language explanation:** The macOS system framework responsible for recording, playback, and audio device management.

**When you may see it:** Console logs, audio error messages, or technical troubleshooting material.

## Bluetooth and device identifiers

### ATVV

**Plain-language explanation:** A technical protocol some Bluetooth voice remotes use to transmit speech. Regular app screens call it the remote voice service.

**When you may see it:** Console logs, Bluetooth protocol material, or advanced troubleshooting.

### UUID / unique device identifier

**Plain-language explanation:** A unique number macOS uses to distinguish a device or Bluetooth service. It is not the device's display name.

**When you may see it:** Console logs, system reports, or Bluetooth technical material.

## macOS permissions

### Input Monitoring

**Plain-language explanation:** Allows the app to read remote-control buttons. The app does not use it to record the text you type.

**When you may see it:** System Settings → Privacy & Security → Input Monitoring.

### Accessibility permission

**Plain-language explanation:** Allows the app to turn remote buttons into shortcuts and send them to the current app.

**When you may see it:** System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility.

## Installer files

### DMG

**Plain-language explanation:** A common Mac disk-image download. You normally open it and drag the app into Applications.

**When you may see it:** Downloading and installing the app.

### PKG

**Plain-language explanation:** A Mac installer with a guided installation flow, commonly used for drivers or components that need to be placed in system locations.

**When you may see it:** Installing a compatible microphone or another audio driver.
